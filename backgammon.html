How to play
  • White moves counter-clockwise toward the bottom-right home board; Black moves the other way toward the top-right. Roll, then move one checker per die (doubles give four moves).
  • Tap a checker, then a highlighted point. One checker can use both dice in a row. Use every die you legally can; if only one can be played, it must be the larger when possible.
  • You can't land on a point holding two or more rival checkers. Landing on a lone one (a blot) hits it onto the bar, and it must come back in through your home board before anything else moves.
  • Once all your checkers are home, bear them off with exact rolls (or higher rolls from your farthest-back point). First to bear off all fifteen wins.
  • Doubling: before rolling, offer to double the stakes. The other side takes (and owns the cube, so only they can double next) or drops and loses at the current value.
  • A gammon (the loser has borne off none) counts double; a backgammon (they also still have a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board) counts triple.
